🎯 The reframe that matters

Altea offering $3M for GrabHC's medical-director and provider contracts is not just an offer — it's a price signal for what a book like this is worth. Sarai's whole playbook is rolling up small physician groups and putting them on his own EMR. We can run that playbook too — and unlike him when he started, we already own the three hard pieces: an EMR (Togo), the management machinery (SEGE onboarding/credentialing/checklists), and a scored acquisition pipeline (14,700 CMS-rated SNFs, owner rollups, 258k NPPES providers). What we lack is his capital and speed. So the real question isn't cash-vs-stock — it's sell the book, or use it as the first block of our own roll-up.
🤝 WITH Altea Sell GrabHC · $3M · asset sale + stock transfer

What we get

  • $3M in cash and/or stock, mix our choice.
  • A working post-acute EMR (Aarista) we don't build, staff, or certify.
  • Exit from the day/night-group operating burden — PMG and the day groups can wind down.
  • Capital to fund the Togo pivot to home-based care — mobile MAs + staff, the "Uber of medicine."
  • Optional consulting income (marketing / clinical / EMR) — but see the non-compete trap.
  • If we take stock, upside participation in a company growing faster than us.

What we give up

  • The SNF footprint — and with it Togo's natural reference customer for its own EMR.
  • The medical-director relationships, which are the stickiest asset in this business.
  • Strategic independence — we hand a physician group to a company that competes with Togo (EMR) and SEGE ("Aarista Legacy — physician services and medical directorship").
  • Clinical/operational data visibility flows to a competitor.
  • Likely closure of PMG, SEGE, and the day groups (SEGE could survive to run Venza/Sweetwater/InstaMobile).

Partnership upside — and the blockers

  • Hybrid on-call: InstaMobile in his facilities + Altea telemedicine after the provider leaves. Blocker: Sarai says he doesn't need InstaMobile.
  • Clinical-quality layer: Togo surfaces preventive-care + CCM guidance inside Aarista, schedules preventive visits. Blocker: he believes Aarista already does this. But you can prove otherwise — you have access to his own physicians who underperform Togo on preventive care. This is the one partnership backed by evidence, not argument.

Honest risks

  • Stock is priced off his own unvalidated $500M. If inflated, the stock quietly delivers a fraction of $3M.
  • Aarista was founded 2023 — betting GrabHC's documentation on a ~3-year-old platform.
  • Consulting roles likely carry non-competes that can undo a clean sale.
  • He grows faster; if we stay in the market we're now competing against our own former book.
🛠 WITHOUT Altea Keep GrabHC · run his playbook · roll up practices ourselves

What we do instead

  • Buy small practices and medical-director books — exactly what he does. Fragmented market, motivated sellers, and we know the price ($3M is his mark for a book like ours).
  • Target SNF owners, not facilities — your own thesis, and the stickier motion. Our data already ranks owner groups (one has 66 facilities / 8,839 beds with acute staffing pain).
  • Put every acquired group on Togo's EMR — each acquisition makes our own product stronger instead of his.
  • Keep InstaMobile growth (Venza, Sweetwater) under SEGE.
  • Sell the preventive-care/CCM advantage as our wedge — the gap we can document in his physicians is a market-wide gap.
  • ALF later, honestly: he's weak there, we're weaker — an open market, not a current edge.

What we already own that he had to build

  • An EMR (Togo) — he needed Aarista; we have ours.
  • The ops machine — SEGE onboarding, 8-phase credentialing, facility go-live checklists, AI file verification. Real infrastructure, already running.
  • A targeting engine — 14,700 scored SNFs, owner rollups, 3,005 ALF prospects, 258k providers for staffing. This is an acquisition pipeline.
  • Entity scaffolding — 28 MSO/PC entities across 9 states, day/night groups already formed.

What it costs us

  • No $3M. The roll-up is self- or debt-funded, and slower.
  • We keep the operating burden: recruiting, credentialing, coverage, turnover.
  • We must finish and certify our own EMR — real engineering cost and time.
  • We race a better-capitalized competitor who is compounding while we build.
  • Execution risk sits entirely with us; no partner de-risks it.

Why this can win

  • Owner relationships compound. Reach enough SNF owners and we can displace his group in those buildings regardless of whose EMR is better.
  • Every acquisition is worth more to us than to him — it adds contracts and a reference customer for Togo's EMR.
  • No non-compete, no dependency, no counterparty risk.
  • If GrabHC's book is worth $3M to him, a portfolio of similar books is worth multiples — to us, not to his cap table.

🖥 Aarista — Altea's EMR confirmed

AI-first post-acute EHR (AI engine "Aari"), founded 2023, Bellingham WA / San Diego. Altea = founder/CEO Jodhvir Sarai, selling post-acute technology and provider services.

Why using it is a pro

A functioning post-acute EMR we don't build, staff, or certify — plus their ONC/uptime/support burden. If we hold stock, its success is partly ours.

Why it's a con

It competes with Togo's EMR, and "Aarista Legacy — physician services and medical directorship" competes with SEGE. Our own PCC dataset already classifies Aarista as competition. Adopting it costs us the reference customer Togo's EMR needs.

Nuance worth pinning down: Aarista appears in the PCC marketplace as an integration, implying it's a provider-side chart syncing with PointClickCare rather than replacing the facility chart. If so, "we'd have to use their EMR" means provider documentation only — facilities keep PCC — and the EMR objection shrinks a lot. Ask for the ONC certification ID, uptime history, a live demo, and a reference customer.

🚫 The non-compete trap

Structure is an asset sale with stock transfer: GrabHC sells the medical-director and provider contracts and folds under Altea. You intend no non-compete in the sale — correct and essential, since SEGE must keep running Venza, Sweetwater, and InstaMobile.

⚠️ Two goals in direct conflict

The marketing / clinical / EMR consulting roles you'd want almost always carry their own non-compete — quietly undoing the clean sale. Treat sale and consulting as one negotiation: (1) scope any restriction to the specific service only, never post-acute or EMR generally; (2) carve out by name SEGE, Venza, Sweetwater, InstaMobile, Togo's EMR, and the home-based business; (3) cap term + geography; (4) ensure non-solicit doesn't block recruiting providers for SEGE's own groups. If a role pays $150k but blocks a business line worth multiples of that, decline the role and keep the freedom. A non-compete would also kill the entire WITHOUT-Altea column — which is exactly why it must be settled first.

🧭 How to decide

Sequence, not simultaneity. (1) Get the claims reconciled — especially $75M-profit-vs-$500M-valuation, which also gives you the real profit-per-book number. (2) Settle the non-compete across sale and consulting; without freedom to compete, the right column doesn't exist and you have no leverage. (3) Validate cost-per-practice and profit-per-practice with one real acquisition conversation. (4) Only then choose sell-vs-build, and if selling, the cash/stock split.

The asymmetry to keep in view: cash is certain, stock is a bet on his execution priced at his own mark, and the roll-up is a bet on our execution priced at market. You control one of those three.

Our strongest card is not GrabHC — it's the documented preventive-care gap in Aarista's own physician base. It's leverage in the sale, the basis for the one partnership he can't dismiss with data in hand, and proof the roll-up thesis has a real clinical wedge. Consider running that comparison before answering him.
