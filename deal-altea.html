🖥 The EMR question — the crux

Why this cuts both ways

Altea is not a neutral acquirer — it is a direct competitor on both fronts: its EMR (Aarista) competes with Togo Health, and its "Aarista Legacy" physician-services/medical-directorship arm competes with SEGE. If GrabHC's SNF care moves onto Altea's EMR, you gain a working platform you don't have to build — but Togo Health's own EMR loses the very SNF footprint it would use as its reference customer, and a competitor gains visibility into your clinical operations. Taking stock makes their EMR's success partly your upside; taking cash keeps you free to sell Togo's EMR against them.

✅ Pro — using their EMR

  • A post-acute EMR you don't have to build, staff, or certify — immediate relief for GrabHC's SNF documentation.
  • They carry the ONC certification, uptime, and support burden.
  • If you hold stock, every dollar their platform earns is partly yours.
  • Integration/interop work becomes their problem, not Scott's roadmap.

⛔ Con — using their EMR

  • Strategic conflict: Togo Health is building an EMR to sell to provider groups. Adopting a competitor's EMR for your own SNF care undercuts that pitch.
  • Lost reference customer: GrabHC is the natural proof-of-concept for Togo's EMR. Hand it over and you lose the showcase.
  • Data & visibility: your clinical/operational data runs through a company that competes with Togo.
  • Switching cost & lock-in: migrating off later is expensive; your workflows get shaped by their roadmap.
  • ALF wedge weakens: our PCC analysis says the opening is software for facilities priced out of PCC — harder to pursue while standardized on Altea.

⚖️ Cash vs. stock — the real tradeoffs

✅ Why more CASH

  • Certain and liquid today. $3M in hand beats $3M of paper that may never convert.
  • No dilution, no preference stack. Private-company common stock usually sits behind investor preferred — in a modest exit you can get little or nothing.
  • Funds your own roadmap — Togo's EMR, the ALF product, more day/night groups — instead of funding theirs.
  • Keeps you a free agent to compete, partner, or sell elsewhere.
  • No dependence on Altea's $500M mark being real.

📈 Why more STOCK

  • Upside participation if Altea grows — the only way this becomes worth more than $3M.
  • Alignment — if you must use their EMR anyway, owning a slice turns a dependency into a shared interest.
  • Possible tax deferral if structured as rollover equity (CPA must confirm — cash is generally taxable at close).
  • Relationship & optionality — a seat at the table, information rights, future partnership.
  • Signals confidence, which can improve the rest of the terms.

❓ Questions to get answered before you choose a mix

On the equity itself

1. Is the $500M a priced round by outside investors, or an internal/founder mark? Pre- or post-money? Who led it and when?

2. What class of shares do we receive — common, or the same preferred as investors? What is the liquidation preference stack ahead of us (and is it participating)?

3. Fully-diluted share count, option pool, outstanding SAFEs/notes — so 0.6% is 0.6% of what, exactly?

4. Information rights, tag-along/drag-along, ROFR, and any transfer restrictions or lock-up.

5. Revenue, burn, and runway. A $500M mark on modest revenue is a very different risk than on large revenue.

On the EMR

6. Are we contractually required to use their EMR for GrabHC SNF care — and for how long? Is that in the purchase agreement or just expected?

7. What is the EMR — built in-house or licensed? Is it ONC-certified? (See research note below.)

8. Does any term restrict Togo Health from selling its own EMR to other provider groups (non-compete / exclusivity)? This is the one that could quietly kill a core business line.

9. Who owns the clinical data, and can we export it if we exit?

On the deal shape

10. Asset or stock sale? Escrow/holdback size? Earn-out tied to what metrics? Employment/non-compete for providers? Which entity is actually being sold — and does it carry the SNF contracts?

That's a much smaller commitment than replacing facility systems, and it's worth pinning down precisely, because it changes how much the EMR "con" actually costs you.

🧭 How to think about the mix

The honest framing: cash is a known quantity; stock is a bet on Altea's execution that you cannot control, cannot easily sell, and that sits behind their investors in a payout. A common approach is to take enough cash to cover what you actually need (fund Togo's EMR, the ALF product, reserves, taxes) and treat any stock as venture-risk money you can afford to see go to zero — then size the stock to whatever passes that test. But the right number depends on facts this page can't know: your tax position, cash needs, and the answers to the ten questions above.

The strategic question is arguably bigger than the mix: if any term restricts Togo Health from selling its EMR, that cost may dwarf the difference between cash and stock. Settle that before optimizing percentages.
