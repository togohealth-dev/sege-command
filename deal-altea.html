🖥 The EMR question — the crux

Why this cuts both ways

Altea is not a neutral acquirer — it is a direct competitor on both fronts: its EMR (Aarista) competes with Togo Health, and its "Aarista Legacy" physician-services/medical-directorship arm competes with SEGE. If GrabHC's SNF care moves onto Altea's EMR, you gain a working platform you don't have to build — but Togo Health's own EMR loses the very SNF footprint it would use as its reference customer, and a competitor gains visibility into your clinical operations. Taking stock makes their EMR's success partly your upside; taking cash keeps you free to sell Togo's EMR against them.

✅ Pro — using their EMR

  • A post-acute EMR you don't have to build, staff, or certify — immediate relief for GrabHC's SNF documentation.
  • They carry the ONC certification, uptime, and support burden.
  • If you hold stock, every dollar their platform earns is partly yours.
  • Integration/interop work becomes their problem, not Scott's roadmap.

⛔ Con — using their EMR

  • Strategic conflict: Togo Health is building an EMR to sell to provider groups. Adopting a competitor's EMR for your own SNF care undercuts that pitch.
  • Lost reference customer: GrabHC is the natural proof-of-concept for Togo's EMR. Hand it over and you lose the showcase.
  • Data & visibility: your clinical/operational data runs through a company that competes with Togo.
  • Switching cost & lock-in: migrating off later is expensive; your workflows get shaped by their roadmap.
  • ALF wedge weakens: our PCC analysis says the opening is software for facilities priced out of PCC — harder to pursue while standardized on Altea.

⚖️ Cash vs. stock — the real tradeoffs

✅ Why more CASH

  • Certain and liquid today. $3M in hand beats $3M of paper that may never convert.
  • No dilution, no preference stack. Private-company common stock usually sits behind investor preferred — in a modest exit you can get little or nothing.
  • Funds your own roadmap — Togo's EMR, the ALF product, more day/night groups — instead of funding theirs.
  • Keeps you a free agent to compete, partner, or sell elsewhere.
  • No dependence on Altea's $500M mark being real.

📈 Why more STOCK

  • Upside participation if Altea grows — the only way this becomes worth more than $3M.
  • Alignment — if you must use their EMR anyway, owning a slice turns a dependency into a shared interest.
  • Possible tax deferral if structured as rollover equity (CPA must confirm — cash is generally taxable at close).
  • Relationship & optionality — a seat at the table, information rights, future partnership.
  • Signals confidence, which can improve the rest of the terms.

❓ Questions to get answered before you choose a mix

On the equity itself

1. Is the $500M a priced round by outside investors, or an internal/founder mark? Pre- or post-money? Who led it and when?

2. What class of shares do we receive — common, or the same preferred as investors? What is the liquidation preference stack ahead of us (and is it participating)?

3. Fully-diluted share count, option pool, outstanding SAFEs/notes — so 0.6% is 0.6% of what, exactly?

4. Information rights, tag-along/drag-along, ROFR, and any transfer restrictions or lock-up.

5. Revenue, burn, and runway. A $500M mark on modest revenue is a very different risk than on large revenue.

On the EMR

6. Are we contractually required to use their EMR for GrabHC SNF care — and for how long? Is that in the purchase agreement or just expected?

7. What is the EMR — built in-house or licensed? Is it ONC-certified? (See research note below.)

8. Does any term restrict Togo Health from selling its own EMR to other provider groups (non-compete / exclusivity)? This is the one that could quietly kill a core business line.

9. Who owns the clinical data, and can we export it if we exit?

On the deal shape

10. Asset or stock sale? Escrow/holdback size? Earn-out tied to what metrics? Employment/non-compete for providers? Which entity is actually being sold — and does it carry the SNF contracts?

🔍 The internal inconsistency worth pressing on

750 facilities × $100k = $75M/year of profit. Against a $500M valuation that is only 6.7× profit — implausibly cheap for a fast-growing healthcare technology company (comparables run far higher). Either the $100k is potential/gross rather than realized net profit, the 750-facility count is aspirational, or he is deliberately underpricing himself — and the last is unlikely from someone forecasting $1B. The most probable read: the per-facility profit figure is not actual net profit. Ask him to reconcile the two numbers; his answer tells you a lot about every other figure.

🔀 Strategic scenarios — what Togo/SEGE become

A · Sell GrabHC, pivot Togo to home-based care

Adopt Aarista for SNF work and redirect Togo's EMR effort into a home-based app connecting doctors to patients with mobile MAs/staff — the "Uber of medicine." This reframes the EMR "loss" as a deliberate exit from a crowded market into a bigger one. Upside: stops competing with Aarista head-on; the mobile-staff logistics layer is genuinely differentiated and nobody owns it. Cost: likely closing PMG, SEGE, and the day groups; a consumer/home model is a different business (payer mix, patient acquisition, unit economics) than facility contracts, and the $3M becomes the seed for it. InstaMobile can likely continue.

B · Hybrid partnership — InstaMobile + Altea telemedicine

Run InstaMobile in Altea facilities, with Altea's telemedicine covering on-call after the provider leaves. Genuinely complementary on paper. Blocker: Sarai says he doesn't need InstaMobile. Don't spend negotiating capital here until he sees a gap he cares about — and he currently doesn't.

C · Togo as the clinical-quality layer inside Aarista

Togo surfaces preventive-medicine and chronic-care-management guidelines inside his EMR, and schedules preventive visits for his providers. Blocker: Sarai believes Aarista already does this. But you have something better than an argument — you have access to physicians using Aarista today who perform poorly on preventive care versus Togo. That makes this the one partnership you can prove rather than pitch. Recommendation: bring evidence, not claims — a small blinded comparison (preventive-service completion, CCM enrollment, gap closure) on his own users. If the gap is real, it reframes you from vendor to necessary capability, and it's leverage in the deal itself. If he still won't look, that's useful information about partnering with him at all.

D · Stay independent and out-grow him via owners

Your own counter-thesis, and it deserves weight: partnering with SNF owners is stickier than selling to facilities. Owner relationships compound — our own targeting data ranks owner groups (e.g. one group with 66 facilities / 8,839 beds and acute staffing pain), and reaching enough owners could eventually displace his provider group regardless of who has the better EMR. Cost: you're racing a much faster-growing, better-capitalized competitor, and SEGE keeps carrying the day/night-group overhead.

On ALF — be honest about the edge

Altea is in ALF and not strong there; Togo is weaker still. So ALF is not a reason to do or avoid this deal today. It's an open market neither of you has won — which is an argument for the ALF-software wedge as a later play, not a differentiator you can claim now.

🚫 The non-compete trap

Structure is an asset sale with stock transfer: GrabHC sells the medical-director and provider contracts and folds under Altea. You intend to keep no non-compete in the sale — correct, and essential, since SEGE must keep running Venza, Sweetwater, and InstaMobile growth.

⚠️ These two goals are in direct conflict

You also want marketing, clinical-consulting, and EMR-consulting roles — and those almost always carry their own non-compete/non-solicit. So a clean sale agreement can be quietly undone by the consulting contracts attached to it. Treat them as one negotiation, not two. Concretely: (1) keep any consulting non-compete narrowly scoped to the specific services delivered, never to post-acute or EMR generally; (2) carve out by name SEGE, Venza, Sweetwater, InstaMobile, Togo's EMR, and the home-based/mobile-staff business; (3) cap duration and geography; (4) make sure non-solicit doesn't stop you recruiting providers for SEGE's own groups. Price the consulting income against what it restricts — if a role pays $150k/yr but blocks a business line worth multiples of that, decline the role and keep the freedom.

🧭 How to think about it

On the mix. Cash is a known quantity; stock is a bet on Altea's execution priced off his own unvalidated mark. That asymmetry is the whole game: if the $500M is inflated 2–3×, stock quietly delivers a fraction of $3M in real value, while cash delivers exactly $3M. Move the haircut slider above to see it. A common approach is to take enough cash to cover what you actually need — funding the Togo pivot, reserves, taxes — and size stock only to what you could watch go to zero. But your tax position and cash needs decide that, and this page can't know them.

On the strategy — the bigger question. The mix matters less than which future you're buying. Selling GrabHC is coherent if it funds a real pivot (scenario A) or buys a provable partnership (scenario C). It's much weaker if it just removes your SNF footprint while you keep competing in the same market with less. The strongest asset you hold isn't GrabHC — it's the documented preventive-care gap in Aarista's own physician base. That's leverage in the deal, the basis for partnership C, and evidence for scenario D.

Sequence I'd suggest: (1) get the claims ledger answered, especially the $75M-profit-vs-$500M-valuation reconciliation; (2) settle the non-compete across sale and consulting together; (3) only then choose a cash/stock split. Optimizing percentages before 1 and 2 is optimizing the wrong variable.
